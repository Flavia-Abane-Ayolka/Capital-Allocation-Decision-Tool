Capital Allocation Decision Tool

A plain-language tool for deciding where to put your money. Enter what a project costs and what you think it'll bring in — it projects five years of cashflow and tells you whether it's worth it, the return on your money, how fast it pays back, and which option to do first. No finance background needed.

Executive summary

Compare & decide

What do these words mean?
Value created (NPV)
What all the future cash is worth in today's money, after allowing for time and the return you asked for. Above $0 means the cash you expect is worth more than what you put in. Higher is better.
Return on capital (ROC)
The average yearly cash the project brings in, as a percentage of what you put in. A quick read on how hard your money works. Compare it with your required return.
Pays back in
Roughly how many years of average cash benefit it takes to get your upfront money back. Shorter is safer.
5-year projection
Your best estimate of the cash in and out each year. Year 0 is the money you put in now; Years 1–5 are the net cash you expect back. If you set a yearly growth rate, later years rise (or fall) from the first.
Labour, running costs & shrinkage
The estimator subtracts wages, materials/fees, and stock lost to waste, breakage or theft, so the yearly benefit is what's actually left over.
Why the highest value isn't always first
A cheaper quick win, or a repair you can't avoid, can reasonably come before the option with the biggest number. The tool organizes the decision; you still make the call.

How it works

  1. Describe the decision — what you're considering, and why.
  2. Estimate the yearly cash benefit — extra income and savings, minus labour, running costs and shrinkage. Use the built-in estimator if you're not sure.
  3. The tool works out the value created (NPV) and how fast it pays back.
  4. Complete the five-point investment-readiness review.
  5. See the return on capital, and whether it clears the target return you set.
  6. Compare the option side by side with your other alternatives.
  7. Make and record the decision.
  8. After you invest, come back and compare actual results with what you expected (the Excel version includes a post-investment review for this).
Evaluate → Decide → Implement → Measure → Learn → Improve — reviewing after you invest is how the next decision gets better.

About the developer
